# Generative AI in Asset Management: **Alpha is real — but it’s not “plug and play”**

(A CAIO’s read of the evidence — and what leaders should do next)

Over the last two years, I’ve heard the same two claims repeated in finance and analytics circles:

1. **“GenAI will level the playing field because everyone can access it.”**
2. **“GenAI is hype — it won’t generate durable performance.”**

A rigorous new academic paper, **“Generative AI and Asset Management” (Aug 2025)**, suggests both statements are incomplete. The data driven reality is more nuanced — and more actionable for anyone building AI strategy in a regulated, high stakes environment.  /

Below is my CAIO take: what the authors found, why it matters, and how I’d translate it into a practical roadmap.

***

## 1 Adoption wasn’t gradual — it was a **step change**

The paper documents a sharp rise in hedge funds’ GenAI usage after the release of GPT 3.5 and ChatGPT era capabilities. 

After adjusting for a low false positive rate, they estimate:

* **approximately 21 percent** of hedge funds adopted GenAI in **2022**   
* **40 percent plus** in **2023**   
* approaching **approximately 60 percent** by **2024** 

**CAIO lens:** That’s not “innovation theater.” That’s an operating shift. In enterprise terms: once adoption crosses a threshold, competitive advantage moves from **whether** you use GenAI to **how well** you operationalize it.

***

## 2 The performance effect is economically meaningful — **for hedge funds**

The headline result: hedge funds that adopt and rely more on GenAI show **higher raw and risk adjusted performance** in the post ChatGPT period. 

In their difference in differences design, an **interquartile increase** in “GenAI Reliance” corresponds to **approximately 2 to 4 percent higher annualized abnormal returns** (depending on the factor model. 

**Important nuance:** this performance benefit **does not generalize cleanly** to non hedge fund asset managers in the same way. 

**CAIO lens:** GenAI isn’t a universal performance booster. It’s an amplifier — and amplifiers reward firms with the right inputs: talent, data, integration, and speed to decision.

***

## 3 The clever part: they measure GenAI usage via “decision influence,” not logins

One of my favorite contributions is methodological.

Because direct telemetry on “who uses GenAI” is rarely observable, the authors build a proxy called **GenAI Reliance** using 13F holdings and ChatGPT generated signals from earnings call transcripts. 

In plain English, the measure asks:

> **How much more of a fund’s portfolio changes can be explained when adding AI generated information, beyond standard fundamentals?** 

**CAIO lens:** This is exactly the direction I encourage organizations to move:  
Do not measure GenAI adoption by “usage.”  
Measure it by **decision impact** and **incremental explanatory power**.

In business analytics terms: the question is not “Did people open the tool?” but “Did the tool change outcomes?”

***

## 4 Where GenAI helps most: **firm specific understanding**

The authors decompose AI signals into three groups:

* Macro  
* Firm policy  
* Firm performance 

They find GenAI’s performance link is strongest for **firm level policy and performance** signals — that is, where the information is unstructured, nuanced, and requires interpretation at scale. 

**CAIO lens:** This aligns with what I see across domains: GenAI tends to shine when the bottleneck is *human attention on messy text*, not when the task is already structured and well modeled.

***

## 5 Talent is a force multiplier (and this is where inequality can widen

Even though tools like ChatGPT are broadly accessible, the paper finds not everyone benefits equally.

Larger, older, more active hedge funds extract more outperformance from GenAI, while smaller or less active firms don’t show the same gains. 

They also show the effect is **much stronger** for funds that had hired AI skilled talent prior to ChatGPT (measured via job postings and AI related roles. 

**CAIO lens:** This is the “people + data + tools” triangle in action.  
GenAI reduces friction, but it doesn’t eliminate the need for:

* good data engineering  
* model risk thinking  
* domain expertise  
* workflow design  

If anything, GenAI may **increase disparity** because top firms can integrate it faster and more safely. 

***

## 6 Micro evidence: trades aligned with GenAI signals do better

The paper goes beyond fund level averages and looks at trade alignment.

They find that trades agreeing with GenAI recommendations deliver **approximately 2.57 percent** average quarterly return, versus **approximately 0.35 percent** for disagreeing trades (a **approximately 2.22 percent** gap. 

**CAIO lens:** This suggests GenAI isn’t just summarizing — it’s influencing real portfolio choices, and “AI consistent” decisions are measurably different.

***

## 7 Market implications: efficiency improves, asymmetry spikes early

The authors also examine market structure effects:

* **Price efficiency improves:** stocks with higher GenAI trade intensity show stronger immediate earnings reactions and weaker post earnings announcement drift.   
* **Information asymmetry initially increases:** bid ask spreads are higher for stocks more exposed to GenAI driven trading early on, especially around earnings announcements, and then fade as adoption expands. 

**CAIO lens:** Every major technology wave has second order effects. If GenAI changes information processing speed and interpretation quality, it can reshape microstructure dynamics — even if only temporarily.

***

# A CAIO playbook: what I would do with these insights

## A Build **workflow integration** before scaling

The survey portion highlights that many hedge funds find workflow integration and in house expertise challenging.  
That’s consistent with most enterprises: the hard part is not “the model,” it’s **the operating system around it**. 

**Action:** pick 1 to 2 workflows where GenAI is naturally strong:

* earnings call summarization and extraction  
* “what changed since last quarter” analysis  
* policy intent signals (capex, hiring, pricing 

## B Invest in **AI literacy and evaluation**, not only tools

If talent multiplies value, the strategic move is to develop:

* analyst training on prompting and verification  
* evaluation harnesses (accuracy, stability, bias  
* feedback loops from decisions to model improvements 

## C Measure adoption as **decision influence**

Take inspiration from “GenAI Reliance”:

* track how often GenAI outputs are cited in investment memos  
* quantify time to insight improvements  
* attribute incremental performance to GenAI assisted signals 

## D Treat governance as an accelerator, not a brake

The paper notes concerns like outages, reliability, and integration challenges.  
In regulated environments, governance must be designed to enable safe speed:

* approved use case catalog  
* data boundaries  
* audit trails  
* human in the loop checkpoints  

***

# Closing thought

GenAI is not “free alpha.” But the evidence suggests it can be a **real edge** when combined with the right **talent**, **data**, and **workflow design** — and that the advantage may concentrate among firms that operationalize it best.
